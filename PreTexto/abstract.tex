\begin{abstractutfpr}
This study investigated the technical feasibility of distributing a Python proof-of-concept application based on the Notification-Oriented Paradigm (NOP) in a Kubernetes cluster. The application was organized into three services that exchange notifications over HTTP/JSON. The experimental campaign compared Docker, Kubernetes with the services on one virtual node, and Kubernetes with the services distributed across two virtual nodes on the same computer. Ten runs per scenario were conducted in matched blocks, each with 1,000 sequential requests. The audit verified all 30,000 HTTP 200 responses and the 7,500 effects predicted by an independent oracle. Median batch times were 18.09 s, 18.35 s, and 19.03 s, respectively. The two-virtual-node scenario had a median paired increase of 1.04 s relative to Docker. The results demonstrate the functional feasibility of the architecture for the tested workload and characterize its execution times on a single-host testbed. The architecture and audit procedure provide a basis for investigating concurrent workloads, distribution across physical machines, and failure recovery.
\end{abstractutfpr}
